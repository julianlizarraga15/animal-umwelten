# An enclosed afternoon patio

## Question

Can the opening read as a patio a person would stay and look at: a closed
place with a floor, walls, and plants growing from soil or pots?

## This experiment

Keep the Rudbeckia, the bee, the web, and the two lessons. Fit the brick walk
and the soil to a three-sided courtyard that includes both camera distances.
Remove leaves that do not meet a stem, a bed, or a pot. Grow vines from the
beds and fill the margins with rooted plants and terracotta pots.

Wall height, bed width, and how far the side walls come forward are tentative
until the opening is reviewed in the browser.

## Acceptance

At the desktop opening, the ground meets walls instead of ending in empty
space, the sides are courtyard rather than void, and no leaf hangs without a
plant. The flower, the bee, the door, the window, and the web stay readable.
The rendering stays an explanation, not a claim of literal animal vision.

## Validation

Look at the opening in the browser, then adjust until the place holds.
Run `npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`, and `npm run check:browser`.

## Result on 2026-11-21

The opening is a three-sided courtyard. Brick paving runs to the walls, soil
beds and pots hold the plants, and the loose canopy leaves are gone. The
Rudbeckia, bee, door, window, and web stay readable, and the two lessons are
unchanged. The upper plaster is still quieter than the reference.

`npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`, and `npm run check:browser` passed.
SwiftShader reports 1,437,730 triangles on the patio frame. Captures are in
`test-results/patio/`. Not committed or deployed.
