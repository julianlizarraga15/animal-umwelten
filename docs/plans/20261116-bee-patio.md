# Explore a patio through one bee-relevant signal

Plan only. No patio, first-person camera, or travel implemented.

## Question and priority

Can a small patio and a first-person inspection point make the documented floral
UV contrast easier to understand than the isolated flower?

Communicating animal perception is the priority. Movement supports inspection.
Fun, challenges, flight realism, and multiple destinations are not requirements.
The broader direction is one shared patio explored through different animals.
This increment includes only the bee. Dog and mouse remain later candidates.

## Smallest experiment

Build one recognizable patio corner with a floor, walls, and a planted pot
containing the existing schematic flower. Reuse Three.js, TypeScript, and Vite.
Use simple geometry with clear scale and lighting, without a new asset pipeline.

Start at a fixed position near the plant, looking toward the flower. The camera
represents a position in the scene associated with the bee. It does not establish
bee eye optics, measured body scale, visual field, or subjective experience.
No visible animal body or third-person introduction is needed for this increment.

Let the visitor look around while stationary and click or tap the flower to
approach automatically. Provide an equivalent HTML button. End at a stable view
of its upper petals, with the documented contrast visible. Offer a return button.

Keep human reference and bee-signal representations at the same camera pose.
This comparison changes the signal encoding, not animal body or camera height.
Propose starting in bee-signal mode, with a clearly labeled human reference for
comparison. Confirm its clarity in the running result.

## Evidence boundary

Reuse the claim and primary sources in [bee vision](../research/bee-vision.md).
That brief documents *Apis mellifera* UV sensitivity and a floral pattern based
on *Rudbeckia hirta*. Their combination here is illustrative.

The upper petal bases absorb more UV than the tips, as documented by
[Horth et al., 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4001241/).
Retain the explicit schematic pattern and explanatory-color labels.
Do not invent UV maps for the floor, walls, leaves, or other patio objects.
Those surroundings provide spatial context and are not a modeled bee color view.

Separate these concepts in the interface and explanation.

- Camera placement and automatic travel are interaction choices.
- Floral UV contrast is the supported signal represented with false colors.
- Other sensory properties are omitted, not implicitly simulated.

## Implementation anchors and sequencing

- `src/main.ts` currently owns rendering, OrbitControls, resize, recovery, and UI
  events. Reuse scene setup and recovery; replace orbit-specific camera behavior.
- `src/flower.ts` owns geometry and `setView`. Retain its scientific invariant.
  Expose a selectable flower target only if hit testing actually needs it.
- `index.html` and `src/style.css` own Spanish controls and responsive layout.
- `scripts/verify-browser.mjs` drives the production page. Update behavior checks
  rather than retaining assumptions about the old camera.

Keep display mode independent of travel state. Start from the existing two-value
`View` type. Use explicit stationary/travel states rather than several booleans.
Finalize the smallest camera contract when implementation begins. Do not add an
animal registry, general navigation engine, collision system, or sensory pipeline.

Throughput checkpoint. Evidence and camera semantics come first. One implementation
owner handles the coupled scene, travel, and comparison. Read-only reviews may run
independently. Any writing delegate uses an isolated worktree. Verify each step
before proceeding; preserve the user's existing uncommitted work.

## Open choices and exclusions

Initial distance, camera height, field of view, look limits, approach duration,
and final framing remain interaction choices to check in the browser. Do not
label them measured animal properties. The first-person default is the proposed
presentation; a brief third-person animal introduction remains optional future work.

Do not add a dog selector with no supported dog experience behind it. Research a
specific second-animal signal before planning that addition. Exclude manual
piloting, missions, scoring, forced discovery loops, extra destinations, and
simulation claims from this increment.
