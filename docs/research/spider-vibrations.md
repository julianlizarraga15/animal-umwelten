# Web-borne vibration sensing

## Documented capability

Hung, Corver, and Gordus (2026) recorded web vibrations and prey-capture
behavior in *Uloborus diversus*. Spiders oriented toward the web radius with
the highest vibration amplitude. Their movements also changed the signals
available during capture. This species naturally constructs horizontal webs.

Source: [Dynamic vibration-driven feedback shapes predator–prey interactions
in an orb-weaving spider](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014802),
especially the abstract, introduction, and prey-localization results.

## Display encoding

Place a schematic horizontal web and spider in the shared patio. In the human
reference, show the threads and insect. In the vibration representation, use
colored threads and pulses to explain the direction of a disturbance.
Touching different parts produces different signal origins. A small challenge
asks the visitor to locate a fly using the highlighted radial direction.
While searching, hide the insect in both representations and reveal it only
after a correct answer. Explain this teaching choice in the interface.

## Limits and inference

The patio placement, four selectable directions, signal speed, palette, body
size, and camera are interaction choices. They are not measurements of this
species. The insect can be hidden in the challenge to focus attention on the
signal; this does not imply blindness. Visible pulses are explanatory graphics,
not literal spider vision, calibrated mechanics, or a reconstruction of animal
subjective experience. The experience does not model active sensing or claim
that amplitude alone explains all prey localization.
