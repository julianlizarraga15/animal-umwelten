# Garden of the Afternoon style sheet

Read from the selected concept's canopy, petals, path, clay, and house crops.
This is an art-direction guide for this scene, not an animal-vision model.

## Shape and detail

- Favor tapered, bowed silhouettes with a visible central fold. Avoid spheres as
  stand-ins for leaves or clusters of identical radial props.
- Keep the frame quiet at thumbnail size: flower and bee first, path second,
  horizontal web third. Architecture stays behind foliage.
- Use broad leaf masses at the edges, grouped midground leaves, and a few small
  blossoms. Leave clear air around the flower head and web.
- Soften transitions through curved surfaces, overlapping forms, and broad light.
  Reserve sharp lines for stems, web strands, and stone joints.

## Color and material

- Lit surfaces use honey ochre, marigold, peach plaster, and dusty terracotta.
- Shadow foliage is deep olive and pine green; sunlit foliage is muted sage.
- Warm light and cooler green shade should separate subjects without clipping
  petal detail or turning the whole frame orange.
- Use matte, rough surfaces. Keep texture broader than a pixel at scene scale;
  use finer marks only in close flower and pot views.
- The UV lesson palette remains a separate explanatory overlay on upper petals.
  No garden material encodes animal perception.

## Interface

- Full-bleed garden, minimal cream typography, dark olive translucent controls.
- Use rounded, lightly blurred surfaces with thin warm borders and visible focus.
- Keep controls in the sky or foreground margin, clear of the flower and web.
- Lesson copy lives in a scrollable side drawer; the scene remains visible behind
  a dimmed backdrop while the native dialog manages focus and Escape dismissal.
- Motion is short and quiet. Reduced-motion users receive the same destinations
  and settled scene.
